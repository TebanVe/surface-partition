# Proposal: Closing the Continuous–Discrete Gap at High N

## 1. The crux, restated

You minimize a functional of a continuous field u, enforce feasibility on one functional of u (column masses, ∫u_k dA = Ā), and grade success on a *different*, discontinuous functional of u (argmax territory). Your own accounting identity (Prop. 1 in `docs/math/07-phase1-wta-balance/main.tex`) makes the gap exact: T_k − Ā = gain_k − lost_k, the net interface-band exchange, which nothing in the energy or constraints controls. At band fraction f_b = 3.72/√(2V/N), each cell's exchange is effectively a random variable with mean ~f_b/2 and dispersion growing with f_b, and validity requires the **maximum over N cells** of its deviation to stay under 5%.

**The single core obstacle: you enforce balance in a representation you don't deliver, and deliver a representation you never constrain — and the residual between them is graded by an extreme-value statistic.** Even if you hold verts-per-cell fixed (plan P3, "γ is N-invariant"), the *worst* cell drifts like σ·√(2 ln N). Any soft, variance-reducing mechanism therefore loses slowly but surely as N→1000. Only mechanisms that make the delivered representation **exactly balanced by construction** are immune to the tail.

There is a second, structural lesson buried in your §4b failure: the territory-aware machinery failed not because "territory can't be enforced" but because you enforced it **inside the nonconvex flow, through the projection**. The trim raises a runt's continuous target d_k, and the iterative projection deposits that mass *nonlocally* (rank-one correction + renorm spreads it over the whole free set), so the argmax flips wherever the deposit lands — disconnected islands. The correct place to enforce discrete balance is where the problem is **convex and local**: the assignment itself.

## 2. Candidate approaches

### A. Balanced readout via per-cell dual shifts (semi-discrete optimal transport at extraction time) — top recommendation

**Mechanism.** Replace ω(i) = argmax_k u_{i,k} with

  ω(i) = argmax_k [ log u_{i,k} + ψ_k ]

where ψ ∈ ℝ^N are N dual variables adjusted so that every cell's discrete territory Σ_{ω(i)=k} v_i lands within tolerance of Ā. This is exactly the Kantorovich dual of the balanced transportation problem: minimize Σ_i v_i·c_{i,ω(i)} with c_{ik} = −log(u_{ik} + η) subject to per-cell capacity Ā. Solve it with entropic Sinkhorn (kernel G_{ik} = (u_{ik}+η)^{1/τ}, marginals v and Ā·1; each iteration is two O(V·N) reductions) or a direct auction/dual-ascent on ψ, then round to a hard assignment. To keep the *entire downstream pipeline unchanged*, don't even hand over labels: define ũ_{ik} ∝ u_{ik}·e^{ψ_k}, renormalize rows to sum to one, and write ũ as `x_opt`. Then argmax(ũ) = the balanced assignment, `ContourAnalyzer`, VP extraction, and Phase 2 run untouched, and edge-crossing positions shift smoothly with ψ.

**Why it closes the gap.** It restores consistency between constraint and readout by moving the *decision boundary* instead of the mass. Raising ψ_k grows cell k's region **monotonically and nested** — new vertices are claimed in order of the log-ratio margin, i.e., outward from the existing core through the adjacent band. That is precisely the locality the trim lacked. And your own forensics say the signal is there: the N=100 runt (cell 92) holds 67% of its mass as a *contiguous halo over its neighbors' adjacent territory* — a per-cell additive offset in log-density space is the exact corrective for "confidently right place, insufficiently loud." Discrete areas are equal up to vertex-mass granularity (max v_i/Ā ≈ 1/verts-per-cell ≪ 5%), **by construction, at any N** — this is the tail-immune property.

**Cost.** Post-hoc, one-shot, zero interference with relaxation dynamics. N=300, V=114k: dense 34M-entry kernel, seconds per Sinkhorn sweep in NumPy, minutes total. At N=1000, V≈1M, sparsify to the top ~10 candidate cells per vertex (u-support + cell adjacency) — same sparsity your deferred scaling plan already needs.

**Cheapest falsifier — runnable today, on existing data, no relaxation run.** Load `x_opt` from the stuck N=300 solution (`results/run_20260714_224821_.../solution/*.h5`), compute ψ, re-run `detect_area_imbalance` and `detect_disconnected_cells` on the shifted argmax. Success: 9 imbalanced → 0 (up to rounding), splits not increased. Refutation: the duals go degenerate because a runt's positive-support area can't reach Ā even through reassignment chains (log this diagnostic per cell: area{u_k &gt; δ} vs Ā), or the shifts manufacture many new islands. Fallback if support binds: blend the cost with geodesic distance to the cell core, c_{ik} = −log(u_{ik}+η) + μ·d²_geo(i, core_k).

**Vs. the rejected list.** It is *not* the trim in disguise: the trim was a primal feedback loop (retarget continuous mass → projection deposits nonlocally → argmax flips remotely → islands, 20× slowdown from killing the plateau). This is a one-shot convex dual solve **after** relaxation ends: no dynamics interference, no wall-time cost, no plateau interaction, and locality via nested boundary growth rather than projection algebra. It is the mechanism your prompt explicitly invites under "change the READOUT."

**Extension (A2), once proven:** if the readout guarantees discrete balance, the *continuous* equal-area constraint becomes optional during relaxation. Weaken it to a soft quadratic (keep sum-to-one + box, which is a closed-form per-vertex simplex projection) and you delete the iterative projection — currently ~86% of Phase-1 runtime. That converts the validity fix into the performance fix. Risk: the hard mass constraint is what keeps cells alive; test at N=100 first.

### B. Volume-constrained multiphase threshold dynamics (MBO with auction/balanced thresholding) as the high-N Phase 1

**Mechanism.** Replace PGD-on-Modica–Mortola with the Merriman–Bence–Osher scheme, in its equal-volume multiphase form (Esedoglu–Otto threshold energies; Jacobs–Kim–Léger "auction dynamics," JCP 2018 — published for exactly this problem class). Iterate: (1) diffuse each indicator for time τ, solving (M + τK) y_k = M χ_k with one prefactorized sparse Cholesky per level; (2) reassign each vertex by **balanced** thresholding — which is literally Approach A applied to y instead of u: ω(i) = argmax_k [y_{ik} + ψ_k] with ψ chosen so all discrete areas equal Ā. The iterate after *every* step is a hard partition with exactly equal discrete areas.

**Why it closes the gap.** It doesn't close the gap — it **abolishes the category**. There is never a continuous field whose readout can diverge; the state *is* the discrete partition, and the scheme monotonically decreases a Lyapunov functional that Γ-converges to the same perimeter limit as your energy (same Modica–Mortola family; the constant is immaterial). Connectivity is not per-step guaranteed, but thresholded diffusion is mean-curvature flow: stray islands are perimeter-inefficient and shrink to extinction — the opposite dynamic to the trim, which manufactured them. Seeded farthest-point init, the mesh ladder, M, K, v, HDF5 formats, and Phase 2 all carry over unchanged; the MBO output is already crisp indicators, which is exactly what `ContourAnalyzer` wants.

**Cost.** Per iteration at N=300, V=114k: one factorization per level (reused), 300 back-substitutions (~seconds) + one balanced assignment (~seconds). MBO converges in tens of iterations, not 30,000. Estimate minutes per level against your current hours-to-days — plausibly 100× faster than Phase 1 *while* being gap-free. Known failure mode: mesh pinning when √τ ≲ h — the same coupling you already respect as ε ≈ h, so set τ ~ h² and inherit your ladder policy.

**Cheapest falsifier.** ~150-line prototype: initialize from your seeded Voronoi labels at the current level-2 mesh (V=47,488), N=300, run 50 MBO/auction iterations, run all three gates plus total perimeter vs the λ=12 PGD solution. Refuted if perimeter is materially worse (&gt;3–5% after Phase 2 polish) or pinning stalls it at achievable τ.

**Vs. the rejected list.** This is the "different partition formulation entirely" your prompt allows, and it differs from rejected item (a) (energy-term augmentation) in kind: nothing is added to a soft energy; the hard assignment *is* the state. It preserves both of your keepers (seeded init; the corrected-well Γ-limit is the same perimeter functional).

### C. Capacity-constrained geodesic power diagram + Lloyd (the geometric route)

**Mechanism.** Drop the field entirely. N sites on the surface (start = your farthest-point seeds), geodesic distances d(i, s_k) via the heat method (one more prefactorized solve) or Dijkstra with edge lengths; assignment = balanced transport with cost d² (again the same dual-shift solver as A — power weights ψ_k make this a geodesic power diagram with prescribed capacities, Aurenhammer/Mérigot); Lloyd step: move each site to its cell's v-weighted centroid; iterate ~30 times. Output labels → Phase 2.

**Why it plausibly works.** At N ≥ 300 the optimal partition is asymptotically the hexagonal honeycomb; centroidal capacity-constrained power diagrams are the standard constructive route to near-hexagonal equal-area cells (blue-noise/quantization literature), typically within a few percent of optimal perimeter — which is Phase 2's job to recover anyway. Power cells of a geodesic metric with cells far smaller than the injectivity radius are connected in practice; residual violations go to the repair stage (E). Exact discrete balance by construction — tail-immune.

**Cost.** N distance computations per Lloyd iteration (back-substitutions or Dijkstras), trivially scales to N=1000. **Falsifier:** one-day implementation on the existing torus mesh at N=300; gates + perimeter vs relaxation. Refuted if perimeter after Phase 2 is consistently worse than the relaxation's after Phase 2 (measure at N=100 where the relaxation is known-good — this comparison is also the "shame test": if CCVT+Phase2 matches relaxation+Phase2 at N=100, the expensive relaxation is not earning its cost at high N). **Vs. rejected:** different formulation; no overlap with any rejected lever. Relative to B: simpler machinery, but optimizes a quantization energy rather than perimeter, so expect slightly worse pre-Phase-2 boundaries (B is C with mobility-aware diffusion in place of isotropic distance).

### D. Geometry/mesh policy: aspect ratio, spatially varying ε, and a coarse-level resolution floor

**Mechanism.** Three config-level moves that shrink the exchange variance at its source. (i) The structured mesh's triangle-area anisotropy is (R+r)/(R−r): 4.0 at your arbitrary r/R = 0.6, but 1.86 at r/R = 0.3 — a global ε = √(mean area) is 2× wrong on both equators at 0.6, and your own data localize the "discretization-artifact" failure subtype in fine-triangle regions. Either reduce r/R or implement per-triangle ε(x) = √(local area) in the K/M weighting (Modica–Mortola constant is ε-independent, limit unchanged — already sketched as P3-optional). (ii) Choose (r/R, N) near-commensurate with a hexagonal lattice on the (approximately flat at high N) torus — fewer forced 5/7 defects means fewer squeezed cells; this is a cheap sweep, not a theory project. (iii) Enforce a **coarsest-level** floor of verts/cell ≥ ~150–300: your §4b data show splits are *born at level 0* (8 → 6 → 3 → 3 across levels) where f_b is huge; at high N, start the ladder at what is currently level 1–2.

**Why it helps / limits.** Pure variance reduction — it cannot beat the √(2 ln N) tail drift, so it is a supporting lever, never the fix. **Falsifier:** N=300, r/R = 0.3 and 0.45, same seed/λ/ladder, count gate failures; separately the level-0-skip run. Config-only except local-ε (small assembly change). **Vs. rejected:** item 3 rejected *finer finest* meshes; this changes *anisotropy*, *commensurability*, and the *coarsest* level — three levers item 3 never touched. Item 4 rejected *uniform* scaling; aspect ratio is explicitly not that.

### E. Discrete repair stage: connectivity-preserving balanced re-labeling

**Mechanism.** Operate on the final labeling only. (1) Island reassignment: for each non-largest component (from `detect_disconnected_cells`), relabel it to the neighboring cell with the longest shared boundary. (2) Rebalance: min-cost flow on the *cell adjacency graph* — deficits/surpluses as demands/supplies, edge cost = current shared-boundary length — then realize each flow unit by advancing the shared boundary vertex-by-vertex (a BFS front from the shared interface into the donor), with an articulation check so no move disconnects the donor. Connectivity is an **invariant of every move**, which is exactly the invariant the trim violated. (3) Optionally a final pass of A restricted to the repaired labels.

**Why.** Any of A/B/C can leave a handful of residual splits; E composes with all of them and also, standalone, might already rescue existing N=300 outputs (9 runts + 2 splits is a small repair problem). Perimeter damage is local and Phase 2 exists to polish. **Cost:** near-zero (graph ops on V=114k). **Falsifier:** run on `run_20260714_224821`; refuted if the boundary-advance flow cannot reach 5% without disconnecting donors (log it). **Vs. rejected:** the trim acted *during* relaxation through the projection; this is post-hoc, combinatorial, and connectivity-invariant by construction — the prompt's "post-processing/repair" free variable.

## 4. Where I think your framing is wrong

1. **"Phase 1 must produce the valid partition."** No — the *handoff* must be valid. Phase 2's iter-0 feasibility is the worst discrete deviation; nothing downstream consumes the continuous masses. Validity is a property of the extraction, and you are grading a relaxation on a functional (argmax) it was never told about. Once stated this way, the 5% area gate becomes trivially satisfiable at readout, and the only real residual gate is connectivity.

2. **The equal-*continuous*-area constraint is neither necessary nor sufficient — and it's your main cost center.** Not sufficient is the whole wall; not necessary follows from A. You pay ~86% of Phase-1 runtime for an iterative projection enforcing a constraint whose delivered meaning is restored elsewhere. A2 (soft mass + simplex projection) is the follow-through; at minimum, stop treating exact continuous mass as sacred.

3. **The λ-window ladder ("needed λ grows with N") is a symptom you keep managing instead of a mechanism you've removed.** Your own plan §2.3 shows the ceiling is an artifact of a frozen optimizer and a non-idempotent projection. With A in place, λ can return to a moderate N-independent value; with B, λ ceases to exist. Do not carry per-N λ tuning toward N=1000.

4. **Extreme-value blind spot in P3/γ-calibration.** "Verts-per-cell fixed ⇒ N-invariant" is a *per-cell* statement; the gate binds on the *max* of N draws, which grows ~√(2 ln N). All soft mechanisms (balance term included, even if the trim is exonerated by your pending balance-only/trim-only ablation) degrade on this axis. Only exact-assignment mechanisms don't.

5. **At high N you may not need the Γ-relaxation at all.** Its marginal perimeter value over a centroidal capacity-constrained diagram shrinks as the optimum approaches the honeycomb, while its cost explodes. Run the C-vs-relaxation comparison at N=100 (both feed Phase 2) before investing further in relaxation-side machinery — if the gap after Phase 2 is &lt;1%, the N=1000 plan should be B or C + Phase 2, with the relaxation retired above N≈200.

6. **A process note with technical content:** the 763-line proof document for the balance term proved six properties of the *term* while the failure came from the *trim's interaction with the projection* — which none of the six propositions addressed. The predictive question for any territory mechanism is not "does the force vanish at balance" but "**through what operator does the correction reach the field, and is that operator local?**" The projection is nonlocal; that alone predicted islands. A's boundary-monotone growth and B's curvature flow both pass this test by construction — apply it as the first filter on any future mechanism.
