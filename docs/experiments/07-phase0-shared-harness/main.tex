\title{\textbf{Qualifying the Measurement Instrument}\\[0.5em]
  \large Phase~0 of the Phase~1 replacement programme --- and five occasions on
  which a measurement artefact was read as a result about the method}
\author{}
\date{August 2026}

\begin{document}
\maketitle

\begin{abstract}
Before approaches \textbf{B} (auction-dynamics MBO) and \textbf{C}
(capacity-constrained geodesic Lloyd) can be compared against the PGD control on
perimeter, the things that would do the comparing must themselves be shown to
work: a \emph{balanced-assignment solver} shared by all three approaches, and a
single \emph{evaluation harness}. This report records that qualification. Four
results. (1)~The harness reproduces the validated \(N{=}100\) deliverable's
Phase~2 perimeter \textbf{bitwise} --- \(185.2546144718457\) --- from a
completely different entry path, which simultaneously establishes that hardening
an arm's labels costs it nothing and that Phase~2 is bit-deterministic on this
host. (2)~The shared solver needed \emph{no new algorithm}: the conclusion that
an auction or Sinkhorn solver was required came from a fixture modelling a state
the arms occupy for one outer iteration. (3)~Under the final protocol
(three meshes \(\times\) two arms \(\times\) three seeds) the solver passes
\textbf{18/18}, and the gate is shown to discriminate --- the pre-0a
un-normalized solver is \emph{indistinguishable from doing nothing}. (4)~Measured
at genuinely equal footing the arms sit at \textbf{parity, \(\pm 10\%\)}, with the
incumbent, not clear of it; two earlier versions of this claim were wrong in the
arms' favour. The methodological content is larger than the technical content:
\textbf{five} times during this work a defect in the measurement was read as a
result about the method, and the report documents what caught each one.
\end{abstract}

\section{Why Phase 0 exists}

The Phase~1 relaxation costs \textbf{22.0--79.3\,h} for a completed \(N{=}300\)
solution and its winner-take-all readout is not a valid partition, so it is
repaired afterwards. That does not scale. Approaches B and C would produce a
valid partition at every step in minutes. Deciding between them requires
measuring \emph{perimeter after Phase~2}, and both --- plus the shipped approach
A --- need one shared primitive: \emph{balanced assignment given arbitrary
per-vertex scores}, which exists inside the balanced readout as
\code{solve\_dual\_offsets}.

Phase~0 makes that primitive usable on foreign score scales (0a), builds one
evaluation harness (0b), adopts the \(N{=}300\) baselines (0c), and proves the
harness reproduces a known answer (0d). \textbf{It measures nothing about
perimeter}; every question about whether B or C is any good remains open.

\section{0d --- the harness reproduces a known answer, bitwise}

The strongest single result. The \(N{=}100\) control's own winner-take-all
labels were hardened to one-hot densities, written in the Phase~1 solution
schema, and pushed through the entire harness under the control's own campaign
\code{refinement.yaml}:

\begin{center}\small
\begin{tabular}{@{}ll@{}}
\toprule
reference & \(185.2546144718457\) \\
harness & \(185.2546144718457\) \quad (iterate 20 of 20) \\
relative difference & \(0.000000\%\) \quad bitwise: \textbf{true} \quad wall 2023\,s \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

Independently verified: the scratch campaign holds 20 fresh iteration files, its
\code{refinement.yaml} names the one-hot arm file as \code{base\_solution}, and
the \emph{entire} 20-iterate trajectory is bitwise equal to the control campaign
computed five weeks earlier, including the 14{,}883-entry
\code{lambda\_parameters} array.

Three consequences. \textbf{(a)}~The apples-to-apples question is settled
exactly: an arm's hard labels and PGD's continuous field produce identical
Phase~2 initial states, because \code{ContourAnalyzer} reads densities only
through \code{argmax} and every variable point starts at \(\lambda{=}0.5\).
\textbf{(b)}~Phase~2 is bit-deterministic on this host, previously an unverified
load-bearing assumption. \textbf{(c)}~The control is itself \emph{censored} ---
its best iterate is its last --- so \(185.2546144718457\) is a \emph{bound}.

\section{0a --- no new solver was needed}

\code{solve\_dual\_offsets} is subgradient ascent on the semi-discrete OT dual.
It is \emph{correct} for any additive score matrix but its step schedule was
calibrated to the log-density scale. Normalizing scores by a robust
assignment-margin scale, and raising the step (\code{normalized\_eta0} \(0.5 \to
2.0\); measured \(3.654 \to 2.209 \to \mathbf{1.672} \to 2.248\%\) for
\(\eta_0 = 0.5, 1, 2, 4\)) and the budget (\(400 \to 2000\)) is the whole fix.
Approach A's path is untouched and byte-identical.

\subsection{The conclusion that was withdrawn}

The first version of gate~2 scored a \emph{cold start} --- balance a raw
geometric guess in one shot --- and B failed at \(45.35\%\). This was written up
as structural: the shipped solver is subgradient ascent tuned for a small
correction, B and C need to rebalance \(\sim\)230 of 300 cells, therefore build a
Bertsekas auction or an entropic solver. \textbf{All of that was wrong.} B and C
are \emph{iterative}: from outer iteration~1 each assignment starts from an
already-balanced partition. An iterated Lloyd loop with the \emph{existing}
solver runs 23 consecutive iterations under the bar. Sinkhorn, implemented and
measured, \emph{loses} to the incumbent on 5 of 6 score matrices. Two knobs
dismissed as dead --- budget and step size --- each fix it on their own; the
``a 10\(\times\) budget costs 20 minutes'' figure was operations \emph{counted},
never timed (the true cost is 33.9\,ms/iteration).

\section{The gate, and whether it discriminates}

\begin{figure}[H]\centering
\includegraphics[width=\linewidth]{fig_final_vs_bar.pdf}
\caption{Final protocol: three fixtures, two arms, three seeds, 18/18 pass.
Red line: the acceptance bar. Grey dashed: the incumbent at equal budget.}
\end{figure}

The bar is \(\max(2\times\text{vertex granularity},\ \text{A's own dual stall at
equal budget})\). A bar the shipped solver itself fails cannot be evidence about
a foreign score scale; a bar below the one-vertex granularity floor is
impossible by construction.

\begin{figure}[H]\centering
\includegraphics[width=\linewidth]{fig_discrimination.pdf}
\caption{Degraded solvers at the production pin, C scores. The pre-0a
un-normalized solver scores \(16.588\%\) --- \emph{equal to the null solver to
three decimals}, because its best iterate is its \(\psi{=}0\) starting point.}
\end{figure}

The shipped configuration sits \(27\text{--}48\%\) under its bars with the
failure boundary a factor \(2\text{--}5\) away. Gate~3 asserts a null solver
\emph{fails}; gate~4 verifies that early stopping is safe inside an iterated loop
(\(3.0\times\) faster for \(+0.008\) points of degradation).

Against the incumbent's \(0.5991\%\) at the production pin, C spans
\(0.573\text{--}0.624\%\) and B spans \(0.537\text{--}0.607\%\):
\textbf{parity, \(\pm 10\%\)}. Earlier drafts of this claim said ``2--3\(\times\)
better'' and then ``clear of parity by 30\%''; both compared arms at 2000
iterations against A at 400. \emph{A budget difference wearing a method
difference's clothes.} The assignment step is not a differentiator and was never
meant to be one --- 0a's job was to make the shared primitive usable so that
perimeter can be measured.

\section{Five measurement artefacts read as method results}

\begin{enumerate}\itemsep2pt
\item \textbf{The stall guard} warned on any floored line search --- which is how
  a healthy level normally ends. Two commits reasoned from the false positive.
\item \textbf{A corpus glob} reached 130 of 350 traces, and the resulting
  ``empty gap'' became a report's strongest argument.
\item \textbf{A baseline audit} enumerated one working directory and concluded no
  \(N{=}300\) Phase~2 baseline existed. Two did, in a sibling worktree.
\item \textbf{The theatre bar}: gate~2's bar included \(1.25\times\) a strong
  reference computed \emph{with the solver under test}, so a broken solver
  inflated its own bar. Six crippled solvers passed, the weakest doing nothing at
  all.
\item \textbf{The early-stop mask}: with early stopping inside the measurement
  path, gate~2 passed 12/12 while every number hugged its bar from below
  (Figure~3).
\end{enumerate}

\begin{figure}[H]\centering
\includegraphics[width=0.82\linewidth]{fig_masking.pdf}
\caption{Artefact 5. A passing gate looks identical either way; only the
pre-registered prediction distinguished them.}
\end{figure}

Two things caught these, and neither was insight. \textbf{Pre-registration}: the
early-stop mask surfaced only because predicted values were committed before the
run, and four ``missed'' in a suspiciously uniform direction. \textbf{Asking
whether anything fails}: every test asked \emph{``does the shipped solver
pass?''} and none asked \emph{``does anything fail?''}, so a gate that passed
everything looked exactly like a gate that worked. Gate~3 now closes that.

\paragraph{On ``survived adversarial review''.} Four adversarial reviews inform
this work and each found real defects. They were subagents dispatched
\emph{from the same session as the work they reviewed} --- independent of the
author's reasoning, not of the author's framing, tooling, or choice of what to
point them at. Every load-bearing number they produced has since been
self-reproduced, except one marked indicative in the plan. That caveat belongs
next to the claim.

\section{Conclusions}

\begin{enumerate}\itemsep2pt
\item The harness is qualified: it returns a known answer bitwise, and hardening
  an arm's labels costs nothing measurable.
\item No new assignment solver is needed. Normalization, step size and budget
  suffice; the auction/Sinkhorn conclusion was a fixture artefact.
\item The gate discriminates: 18/18 for the shipped configuration, with the
  pre-0a solver indistinguishable from doing nothing.
\item Arms and incumbent are at parity \(\pm 10\%\) on assignment quality. The
  open question --- whether B or C produces \emph{shorter perimeter} --- is
  untouched by this report.
\end{enumerate}
